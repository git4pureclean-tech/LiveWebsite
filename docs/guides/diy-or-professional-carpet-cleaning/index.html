Care guide

DIY or professional carpet cleaning?

Decide whether a spot cleaner, a rental or a professional visit suits your carpet. Start with the fibre, the size of the job and what caused the mark.

Start with the fibre and the spill

A small, fresh spill on a washable synthetic carpet is a reasonable job for a spot cleaner. Check the care instructions and measure the cleaning solution. Extra detergent can leave a tacky patch that attracts dirt after the carpet dries.

Start by blotting a fresh spill with a white cloth and checking the care label. Follow the cleaner’s directions and test it in a hidden spot. Colour transfer to the cloth or an unknown fibre are reasons to stop and get advice.

Look beyond the surface

If an odour returns as the carpet dries, the spill may have reached the underpad. Describe the original spill when you call. For a hand-knotted, wool or silk rug, ask for fibre identification and a dye test before wet cleaning.

Repeated rinsing can add moisture without dealing with what’s underneath. For a wool rug, check the options for on-site cleaning and rug pickup before using a rental machine.

Compare the whole job

For one lightly used room, compare the machine rental and solution with a professional quote. For several rooms, stairs or heavily worn paths, include the time spent moving the machine and waiting for the carpet to dry. A professional extractor can recover more of the rinse water.

For a quote you can compare with a rental, list the rooms and stairs, flag any recurring stains or odours and ask what spot treatment is included. Our residential carpet cleaning page explains what happens during a visit.

Keep the cleaning record

Check your carpet manufacturer’s care and warranty documents for the required cleaning method and interval. Save the invoice with the product information, especially if you may need to make a claim.

Tell the technician what you already tried, including the name of any cleaning product. This helps them assess the stain and plan a treatment that suits the carpet.
